# The chat page: a look of its own, an open composer, a drawer, and replies as Markdown

## The look

One source: `design/tokens.json` holds every colour, radius and type stack once, for both themes. `node design/build.mjs` writes what the builds import (`design/generated/tokens.css` and `theme.css`, ignored by git) and the small generated files: `checkout/src/checkout/tokens_css.py` (the simulated checkout page), `host/src/chat/palette.py` (the ground colours and the primary, for the browser's theme colour and the manifest), `host/src/chat/static/chat/host-styles.js` (the theme in the names the MCP Apps standard gives a view), the brand assets as SVG (`design/brand/`), the wordmark partial and the icons' SVG sources (`docs/brand.md`). The owner's palette is `brand` in `tokens.json`; a theme colour names one as `"brand.deep"`. `tools/up.sh`, `host/build.mjs` and `card/build.py` run it first. The host page, the approval card, the menu card and the checkout page all read the same custom properties, so a colour is changed in one file. `design/components.css` holds the parts they share (the primary button, the quiet button, the field, the focus ring), written only in token utilities.

The brand is green: the deep green, three graphic greens, mint, warm white and warm charcoal. The name, the assets and the sampled colours are in `docs/brand.md`.

- **Neutrals.** Warm, never pure grey. Light: a warm off-white ground (`#FAF8F2`), a near-white raised surface (`#FEFDFA`), a sunken surface (`#F0EDE4`), hairlines, and a warm charcoal ink (`#221F18`) in three steps, all tinted toward hue 90. Dark is designed separately, not inverted: deep green-black surfaces (ground `#08120C`, raised `#0E1D15`, sunken `#17291F`, hue 160) and a mint-white ink (`#E6F3E8`).
- **Primary: deep green.** `#03492F` in light, sampled from the logo (the warm-white label on it is 10.23:1; hover `#023924` 12.67:1; pressed `#022B1A` 15.0:1; the green itself on ground, raised, sunken and mint is 9.90, 10.33, 8.98 and 9.34:1) and a bright green `#38D080` in dark, which takes dark ink (`#001A0C`, 9.11:1). It is the primary action (Send, Approve, Open checkout, Confirm, Pay on the checkout page), the keyboard focus ring (2px) and the composer's focused edge, and the edge of a selected chip. It is not text, not a link colour and not a status. The primary action is the one solid green fill on a screen.
- **Accent: the brand greens, as graphics.** `accent` is `#00AD53` in light and `#01D879` in dark: the plus of the wordmark. The other greens (bright `#01BA65`, mint `#CBFAE3`, mint soft `#DDF3E6`) live in the symbol and the logo variants (`docs/brand.md`). They fail as text, by design: on white `#00AD53` is 2.96:1, `#01BA65` 2.56, `#01D879` 1.89, mint 1.15, and on the warm ground 2.78, 2.41, 1.78 and 1.08. So they are never small text, a control, or a border that carries meaning; `mark`, `accent` and `mark-mint` appear in one source, the wordmark partial, and the palette test fails anywhere else. The numerals of the wordmark are the `mark` token (the deep green in light, white in dark; at least 4.5:1 on all four surfaces).
- **Mint.** A pale green tint for surfaces and selection: the person's message bubble, the icon tiles and the hover of the starters, the drawer's current chat, a selected chip, the hover of the secondary button (`#E0F7EA` light, `#143525` dark). All three inks are 5.3:1 or more on it.
- **Success is not brand green.** Success and delivered states use a separate teal (hue 195: `#005F5F` on a pale aqua tint `#DDF6F6` in light, `#57D4D4` on `#012D2D` in dark), 34 degrees of hue away from the primary and 41 from the accent, and they are always a soft chip with a check icon and the word ("Airtime delivered", "Transfer sent"), never a solid fill and never a button. The primary is a solid deep green with a label, and only ever an action, so "Approve" beside "Paid" cannot be mistaken for each other: one is a filled button, the other a tinted, outlined-icon status. Warning (amber, hue 70) and failure (red, hue 28) stay separate from both, are also chips with their own icon and word ("Not charged", "Refund due", "Not sent"), and neither is ever brand green. State is never told by colour alone: every status has its icon and its text. The palette test keeps `ok` at least 30 degrees of hue from the primary, the accent and mint, and `warn` and `bad` at least 60 from both greens.
- **Tiles.** Four category tints for the menu's drawn tiles (main in mint, soup in apricot, side in leaf, drink in sky), each with its own ink; nothing else is tinted this way.
- **Contrast.** `conformance/palette.test.mjs` (no stack) measures 59 pairs in each theme (the four `google-*` pairs of the sign-in button included): ink, ink-2 and ink-3 on ground, raised, sunken and mint (4.5:1 or more; lowest 5.13 in light, 5.46 in dark), the label on each step of the primary (10.2 to 15.0 light, 7.3 to 10.8 dark), the primary and the wordmark's numerals against the four surfaces (4.5:1 or more: 6.7 to 18.8), the strong edge against ground, raised and mint (3:1 or more; 3.10 lowest), each status colour on the four surfaces and on its own tint (4.5:1 or more; lowest 5.93) with ink and ink-2 on each tint, and each tile's ink. `design/contrast.mjs` is that list as data. The same test checks that neutrals are tinted, that success sits apart from the brand greens, that the generated files are current, that every colour in the built stylesheets (host, both cards, the checkout page) is a token's, that no source writes a colour or a default Tailwind palette class, that the primary appears only on the primary action, on focus and on a selected edge, that the brand greens appear only as the wordmark's `fill-accent`, `fill-mark` and `fill-mark-mint` and never as text, that none of them reaches 3:1 on white or the ground, and that a vivid green primary would fail its label. `chat-home.mjs`, `card-states.mjs`, `menu-card.mjs` and `sim-checkout.mjs` also measure the contrast of what each page paints in a browser, in both themes.
- **Type.** System stack (`system-ui`, sans) for everything, on purpose: this product's text carries the naira sign and Yoruba tone marks, and Manrope has neither, so the system font would be needed anyway. **Manrope** (SIL Open Font License 1.1, from the Manrope Project, weight axis cut to 500 to 800, Latin subset, 19.4 KB, `host/src/chat/static/chat/fonts/manrope.woff2`, licence text beside it as `OFL.txt`) sets headings in replies and is fetched only by a page that draws one (`font-display: swap`); the home and a conversation without a heading load no web font. Scale: 12 (notes), 14 (tool rows, starters, notices), 16 (the conversation, the composer), 18 (headings in replies); the wordmark is a drawing 6rem tall. Replies at 16/28, at most 65 characters wide. The cards and the simulated checkout page keep the system stack: a card is a sandboxed page with no access to the host's font file.
- **Shape.** Cards and message bubbles 1rem, buttons and fields 0.75rem, the drawer 1.25rem, the composer and the round buttons fully round. Cards in the transcript are drawn on the same raised surface with the same hairline, and their edge lines up with the text column.

## The home

- `/` is the wordmark (the product name "234" from `PRODUCT_NAME`, drawn as an inline SVG: the logo, traced numerals in the `mark` colour with the plus of three pills at the 4's upper right, light and dark through tokens; it is named for a screen reader by the setting), one sentence under it ("I buy airtime, send money and order food, and you approve every payment."), the composer, and the starters. No header, footer, legal line or connector label. The product name is in the tab title, the application-name meta, the manifest, the wordmark, and the composer's placeholder and label ("Ask <name>"), all from `PRODUCT_NAME` in `host/src/config/settings.py` (now `234`). The tagline ("Talk and do anything with 234.") is in the meta description, the Open Graph tags and the manifest, not on the page: it is a slogan that says "anything", and the home says what the product does (`docs/brand.md`). The starters are only the actions that work today.
- **Starters** (`host/src/chat/starters.py`, `<chat-starters>`): six buttons under the composer, each with a `label` (at most 32 characters) and an icon drawn for it (48x48, 3px round strokes, the 20% fill, the language of the menu tiles): "Buy ₦500 MTN airtime", "Buy ₦1,000 MTN data", "Send ₦5,000 to a friend", "Order jollof rice for delivery", "Pay ₦2,500 to Ada Stores", "What can you do?". Two columns on a phone (icon above the words), three from 640px (icon beside them). Each is marked in `starters.py`, in one place, as one of two kinds, and the page reads it from the markup (`data-fill`):
  - **Sends** (food, pay, help): whole messages. A press puts the message in the composer and sends it; every starter is disabled from the first press, so one press is one message however fast it is repeated, and they come back if the send is refused. On a phone (coarse pointer) the keyboard stays down after a tap.
  - **Fills** (airtime, data, transfer): the message lacks something only the person knows, so pressing one sends nothing. It puts the beginning in the composer ("Buy ₦500 MTN airtime for ", "Buy ₦1,000 MTN data for ", "Send ₦5,000 to "), focuses the field with the cursor at the end (on a phone too: the person is about to type), and leaves every starter where it is and usable; another fill replaces the text. What the person types lands after it and Enter or the round button sends that one message.
  The starters are gone once a message has gone and there is a chat, and a new empty home has them again.
- **What each one does with the scripted model and the simulators** (`host/tests/scripted_intents.py`, `conformance/chat-home.mjs`, `conformance/chat-starters.mjs`): pay and food go straight to a card (the approval card; the menu card with the jollof dish); a finished airtime ("Buy ₦500 MTN airtime for 08031234567"), data or transfer ("Send ₦5,000 to 0000000000 Zenith") goes to the approval card in one step, and the same request typed without the number is answered with the one thing missing ("Which number should get the ₦500 MTN airtime?"); the help starter answers from the tools the model was offered ("I can buy airtime, buy data, send money to a bank account, order food and pay a merchant. You approve every payment on a card.") and anything else is turned down with the same list. The scripted model can also play a model that skips the question ("blank phone: Buy ₦500 MTN airtime" calls the airtime tool with an empty number; "blank phone, silent: ..." then says nothing), so the tool rows' handling of a refusal is testable. With a real model this rests on the host's system prompt (`host/src/turns/prompt.py`), built from the connectors that are on; what it says, and what `gpt-oss-120b` did with requests that lack something before and after it, is in [model-behaviour.md](model-behaviour.md).

## The page

- **The composer is one rounded pill** (`static/chat/composer.js`, `<chat-composer>`) with a textarea and one round button, and nothing else in it (no attachments, no microphone, no menu icon: a control that does nothing is not drawn).
  - **Empty page:** the wordmark, its line, the pill and the starters are centred as one group a little above the middle, and the field has focus with no click (`autofocus`).
  - **Docked:** once the first message has made the chat, the pill glides to the bottom edge (260 ms, measured before and after the change and played from the old place with the Web Animations API) and the transcript takes the space. The docked pill has a solid ground under it and a soft fade above it (2rem), so text going under it fades instead of colliding. A reload of an existing chat opens docked, with no animation and without taking the keyboard. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` it is docked at once.
  - **The round button** is the arrow: the neutral surface and disabled while the field is empty or only spaces, the primary colour when there is text. While a turn is open (`chat-thread[data-working]`) it is a stop square in ink, named "Stop". After a press it reads "Stopping" and is disabled until the turn ends.
  - **Keys:** Enter sends, Shift+Enter adds a line. Enter while the field is empty, or while a reply streams, does nothing. Enter that confirms an IME composition does not send (`isComposing`, and `keyCode` 229 as Safari sends it after the composition has ended).
  - **Growth:** the textarea grows with its text to six lines (plus its padding), then scrolls.
  - **Focus:** buttons and links get the 2px primary ring (deep green in light, bright green in dark). The field, which has focus almost all the time, shows the same colour as a 2px edge on the pill, so the brand is on the empty home without a second filled action.
- **The chats button** is a round 44px icon button fixed at the top left (`data-action="chats"`, outside the pill), hidden until the visitor has a chat (the server renders it hidden or not; the first message of a new visitor reveals it). Where sign-in with Google is on ([auth.md](auth.md)) it is there for everyone, because the drawer is the only place to sign in. It sits `max(0.75rem, env(safe-area-inset-*))` from the top and left edge, the transcript starts below it (`4rem` under the inset), and a fade the colour of the ground sits under it so a line scrolling past it fades out instead of running into it. It opens the drawer; it is not in the empty home of a visitor with no chats.
- **The drawer** is a modal `<dialog>` (`chat-sheet`, `static/chat/sheet.js`), 20rem or the screen less 3rem, sliding in from the left in 220 ms (not under reduced motion). Inside: Close, New chat and Share (a chat that exists), a hairline, then the chats newest first, the one on show marked, each with a delete (trash) button; 44px targets throughout. Where sign-in is on, the bottom of the drawer holds "Continue with Google" (Google's own button) or the account (an initial, the email) and "Sign out". Focus moves in, Escape closes it, focus goes back to the button. It is in every page, so opening it costs no request.
- **Scrolling** (`static/chat/scroll.js`): while a reply streams the page follows the newest line. Following is a state, not a guess from the distance at each event: it turns off when the person scrolls up, on when they reach the bottom, and it survives a card growing or a table redrawing (a resize observer keeps the end in view while it is on). When it is off and the end is more than 160px away, one small "Latest" button (44px, above the composer) goes to the end smoothly (at once under reduced motion) and follows again. Sending a message goes to the end.
- **The rest of the transcript:** user messages are a soft neutral bubble (at most 80% wide, right aligned); replies sit on the page without a bubble; a notice is one line with a small icon, in the failure colour when it is an error; "Reconnecting" and an error are one line each above the composer, out of the flow, so nothing jumps. Focus order is: chats button, field, send, starters (empty home), or the transcript (cards, tool rows) then the field.
- **Tool rows** (`_item.html`, `tool-row.js`, `tool-status.js`): one quiet line per call, a native `<details>` closed until the person opens it and never opened by the page: a chevron, the tool's plain name ("Create payment quote"; the connector's own name is the tooltip) and a small word only when the call did not work. A call that worked says nothing.
  - **The word.** `refused` (muted ink), `stopped` (the person pressed Stop) or `failed` (the failure colour). A refused call is `refused` and muted when the model went on in the same turn, which is what lies between two messages from the person: a later call that worked, a card, or words to the person (such as the question that asks for what was missing); the person has nothing to do about it. It is `failed`, the only red, when the turn is over and nothing followed it. While the turn is still open a refusal nothing has followed yet stays muted. The rule is `toolStates` in `tool-status.js` (`conformance/tool-status.test.mjs`); the server renders a stored refusal as the muted `refused` and the page settles it when it loads, so a reload draws what the live page drew. No row says "running": a call is logged with its result, so there is no row before it finishes.
  - **Open.** The arguments as the model sent them and the connector's words, each in a compact code block (12px, `overflow-x: auto`, keyboard-focusable) inside one sunken box no wider than the column; a 130-character argument scrolls inside it on a 320px phone.
  - **Focus.** The summary is a 44px target and draws no outline; the 2px ring with a 2px offset is drawn round the label only (`focus-ring-target`, `design/components.css`), and only for keyboard focus: never on load, and never after a press, in Chromium and in WebKit (a `pointerdown` marks the row `data-pointer`, which suppresses the ring even where a browser calls the focus keyboard focus; a key clears it). The ring starts 6px inside a 390px phone's edge and no ancestor clips it.
- **Phone:** the viewport is `width=device-width, viewport-fit=cover, interactive-widget=resizes-content`, so a browser that resizes the layout for the keyboard (Chrome on Android) shrinks `dvh` and the pill follows. For one that only shrinks the visual viewport (iOS Safari), `keyboard.js` publishes what the visual viewport lost at the bottom as `--keyboard-inset` (0 when pinch-zoomed) and both the empty group's bottom space and the docked pill's `bottom` use it. A long product name wraps inside the field, which grows, and does not push the buttons out (a 30-character product name, as words and as one unbroken word, at 320px).
- Layout: `min-h-dvh`, safe-area insets on the sides, the top and under the composer; the transcript is one column, at most 42rem.
- The chat title is the first 60 characters of the first message; `<title>` shows it.

## After a compaction

When a long chat was compacted (the assistant now reads a summary in place of the older messages, [compaction.md](compaction.md)) the page shows the history unchanged and one quiet line where the compaction sits: "Earlier messages were summarised" (`compaction-note.js`, `_item.html#compaction`), centred between two hairlines in the quiet ink, a 44px native `<details>` closed until the person opens it, and never opened by the page. Opened, it shows the summary in small muted text. Only the latest compaction is marked: a new one replaces the line of the one before, live and on a reload. A compaction that could not be summarised (the fallback, which only trimmed) says "Earlier messages were left out" and has nothing to open. The same rule runs on the server (`history.items`) and in the browser (`thread.js`), and a second tab follows it over the same stream. `conformance/chat-compaction.mjs` runs it in both themes; screenshots `compaction-{light,dark}-1-line.png` and `-2-open.png`.

## Stop

`POST /c/<id>/cancel` (`views/cancel.py`, same access as `send`) asks the chat's Durable Object to stop its turn (`ChatCore.cancel`, `TurnRunner.stop`) and answers `{"cancelled": true|false}` (false: nothing was running). It stops the turn for real: the driver task is cancelled, so the model stream is dropped mid-reply.

- What the model had said is kept as the reply (`assistant` with `finish_reason: "cancelled"`, the text streamed so far, or empty and then not shown). A tool call that was stopped gets a result of its own, "Stopped by the person before it finished.", marked `cancelled`, so nothing runs it again and the model is told the same on its next turn. A call already sent to a connector may have happened there; the log only says it was stopped.
- The turn ends with `turn.finished`, reason `cancelled`, carrying `upto`, the end of the log at that moment. Everything the person had sent by then is settled and not answered; a message that arrives after the stop is answered as usual in a new turn. Cancelled tool results do not ask for a model round (`fold.next_action`).
- A stop that lands before the turn had started still leaves a `turn.started` and `turn.finished` pair, so the message it stopped is not answered by a turn that starts late.
- For an A2A caller such a task reads `TASK_STATE_CANCELED` and is final. A2A's own `tasks:cancel` still answers `TASK_NOT_CANCELABLE`: the endpoint is not wired to this.
- Only the Durable Object runner stops turns. On the measured alternatives (`waituntil`, `queue`) the request answers `cancelled: false`.

## A chat is made by its first message

- `GET /` mints an id and renders the page for a chat that is not stored (`data-draft`). Nothing is written.
- The first message goes to `POST /c/<id>/start`. After the same checks as `send` (clean text, no card number, rate limit) the chat row is made for the visitor and the message goes to the chat's Durable Object. A message that is refused, or a failure in the object, leaves no chat behind.
- The page then behaves as that chat: `history.replaceState` to `/c/<id>/` (no navigation, no history entry), the event stream starts at cursor 0, the drawer gains the row.
- A second message to the same id joins the chat. `claim_chat` (`access.py`) makes that hold for races: D1 reports the loser of an insert as its own exception, not Django's `IntegrityError` (one in twenty double starts answered 500 with `get_or_create`), so any failure is settled by looking the row up again. Someone else's id answers 404.
- A reload of `/` is a new empty composer. A reload of a chat restores it, mid-stream included (the stored partial reply, then the live events).

## Replies are Markdown

One renderer draws a reply, live or stored. The server ships the raw text; `<assistant-text>` (`assistant-text.js`) renders it. User messages, tool rows, card notes and notices stay plain text.

- **Vendored:** `host/src/chat/static/chat/vendor/markdown-it.esm.min.mjs`, markdown-it **15.0.2**, `dist/browser/markdown-it.esm.min.mjs` from the npm tarball, unmodified (sha256 `85feb50fd6ce1b7c49acb02b0337eb622ecc4fc2ed4ae0dcbb8c084556d463b6`), MIT, licence beside it. No bundler. To update: `npm pack markdown-it@X`, copy that file, change this paragraph. The stylesheet build skips the directory.
- **Safety** (`markdown.js`): raw HTML is escaped (`html: false`); links are kept only for `http:`, `https:`, `mailto:` and `tel:` (an allow-list, not markdown-it's block-list, so `//host`, `data:`, `javascript:` and `file:` are all dropped); images are not rendered; every link gets `target="_blank"` and `rel="noopener noreferrer"`; no output carries an inline style (column alignment becomes a class) or a script, so the page policy (`script-src 'self'`, `style-src 'self'`) holds under it. Bare file names such as `main.py` are not linked.
- **Style** (`markdown-style.js`): Tailwind token classes only, in a file the stylesheet build scans; links are ink, underlined (the brand greens are not spent on them); tables sit in a horizontal scroller with `tabular-nums`; long words wrap (`wrap-anywhere`, also on the person's bubble).
- **Streaming** (`assistant-text.js`, `markdown-partial.js`): every change re-renders the whole reply from the accumulated text, at most once per animation frame, and only the blocks that changed are replaced (a scrolled table and a selection survive). While a reply is arriving, `settle()` decides what is drawn: an open `**`, `*`, `_`, `~~` or backtick is closed for now, a marker with nothing after it waits, an unfinished link shows its label, a lone `#`, `-`, `1.` or `>` waits, and a table waits for its header and delimiter row and then grows one whole row at a time. The final text is rendered as it is.
